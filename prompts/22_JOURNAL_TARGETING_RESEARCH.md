# JOURNAL TARGETING RESEARCH - INTENSIVE BEST-FIT AUDIT

## Objective

Identify the journals that are genuinely the best intellectual and practical fit for the manuscript, using current external research rather than memory or generic journal rankings. This module is mandatory in the report front matter whenever web access is available.

The goal is not to maximize prestige. The goal is to answer:

> **Given what this paper actually contributes, which journals are most likely to recognize that contribution as being inside their editorial domain, and what would the author need to satisfy before submitting?**

## 1. Required manuscript map

Before searching journals, extract:

- primary discipline and secondary discipline(s);
- research question;
- paper type: theoretical / empirical / mixed / structural-quantitative / qualitative / experimental / methodological / review / other;
- principal method/design;
- data, population, geography, and period where relevant;
- main contribution type: substantive / methodological / theoretical / measurement / data / replication / synthesis;
- closest literature and closest papers;
- central result and its scope;
- current Submission Readiness verdict;
- manuscript length and unusual format constraints when visible.

Do not start from a list of famous journals. Start from the paper.

## 2. Search strategy - intensive, not superficial

Use current web research. Prefer primary and official sources.

For each serious candidate:

1. Read the journal's official aims/scope or editorial mission.
2. Read current author/submission instructions.
3. Inspect recent issues and recent papers, ideally from the last 3-5 years, to determine what the journal actually publishes rather than relying only on its scope statement.
4. Search for recent articles close in question, method, setting, or contribution.
5. Verify whether the manuscript type is accepted and whether unsolicited submissions are allowed.
6. Verify current fees and distinguish fee types.
7. Verify submission-format and research-transparency requirements.
8. Check whether a published version, working paper, preprint, conference version, or prior submission creates a policy issue when relevant.

Use official journal/publisher pages for fees and author requirements whenever possible. Use Crossref/DOI metadata, official journal issue pages, working-paper repositories, or author pages to verify recent-paper metadata. Do not use crowdsourced fee tables as authoritative when official information exists.

## 3. Candidate generation

Generate a broad internal candidate set, then retain only journals with a defensible connection to the manuscript. Cover neighboring fields when the paper naturally bridges them.

Do not recommend journals merely because:

- they publish social science generally;
- they have a high impact factor;
- the author cited them;
- a famous paper on a vaguely related topic appeared there;
- another paper used the same dataset;
- the journal is a common default in the discipline.

## 4. Fit assessment

For each retained journal assess:

- **Topical fit** - does the journal publish the economic/social-science question?
- **Method fit** - is the paper's empirical/theoretical/qualitative/structural method normal for the journal?
- **Contribution fit** - does the journal value this kind of incremental contribution?
- **Audience fit** - would the paper's natural readers plausibly read this journal?
- **Recent-precedent fit** - are there recent articles sufficiently close to make the recommendation concrete?
- **Format fit** - does the manuscript fit article type/length/appendix conventions?
- **Practical fit** - fees, OA constraints, data/code requirements, and other submission burdens.

Use qualitative ratings **EXCELLENT / STRONG / PLAUSIBLE / WEAK**. Do not create spurious numerical precision unless the product later validates a scoring model.

## 5. Fee taxonomy - hard rule

Never report one generic `fee`. Report these separately:

- **Submission or processing fee** - amount required to have the manuscript considered, if any.
- **Mandatory publication charge** - amount required after acceptance under the standard publication route, if any.
- **Optional open-access APC** - charge only if the author chooses or is required to publish OA.
- **Page/length/color/other author charges** - when applicable.
- **Waivers/discounts/institutional agreements** - only when officially documented; do not assume the user's eligibility.

If an official source does not state a fee, write **NOT VERIFIED**. Never convert absence of information into `$0`. Give currency and verification date.

## 6. Basic submission requirements - minimum fields

For each recommended journal report at least:

- Journal name and publisher/society.
- Official scope fit in one sentence.
- Suitable article type.
- Submission fee.
- Mandatory publication charge, if any.
- Optional OA APC, if relevant.
- Word/page limit or `not stated` / `not verified`.
- Initial-submission format or format-free status.
- Single-/double-/triple-anonymous or other review setup when stated.
- Whether identifying information must be removed.
- Abstract/keyword/classification requirements when stated.
- Data/code/replication requirements.
- Ethics/COI/funding/disclosure requirements material to the paper.
- Preprint/working-paper policy when relevant.
- Whether a cover letter or separate title page is required.
- Whether unsolicited submissions of this article type are accepted.
- Official source(s).
- **Last verified: YYYY-MM-DD.**

Do not bury a prohibitive requirement.

## 7. Recent comparable papers

For every Tier A / strongest candidate, identify 2-4 recent papers that demonstrate fit. They should be intellectually close, not merely from the same broad field. State the overlap:

- same question;
- same mechanism;
- same method;
- same setting/data; or
- same type of contribution.

Do not claim that publication of a similar paper guarantees fit or acceptance.

## 8. Ranking and submission strategy

Return three groups when the evidence supports them:

### TIER A - BEST FIT
Journals with the strongest intellectual/editorial match.

### TIER B - STRONG ALTERNATIVES
Credible targets with a somewhat broader/different audience or contribution threshold.

### TIER C - CONDITIONAL / BROADER ALTERNATIVES
Useful alternatives if Tier A/B are unsuitable because of scope, format, fee, or editorial positioning.

Do not label a journal `safe` unless credible current evidence supports that characterization. Do not invent acceptance probabilities.

Then select exactly:

- **BEST INTELLECTUAL FIT**
- **BEST OVERALL SUBMISSION TARGET**
- **BEST NO/LOW-SUBMISSION-FEE OPTION**, when one can be verified

These can be the same journal. Explain why.

## 9. Readiness interaction

If the paper is **READY FOR SUBMISSION** or **READY AFTER MINOR CORRECTIONS**, provide an ordered submission sequence.

If the paper is **NOT READY**, do not tell the author to submit immediately. Instead state:

> `Journal targets are conditional on resolving the readiness blockers listed above.`

Explain whether fixing the blockers would change the target set. A major redesign can move the paper into a different journal market.

## 10. Cost-benefit and author constraints

Respect optional author constraints such as:

- no submission fee;
- maximum fee;
- no mandatory APC;
- open-access preference;
- disciplinary audience;
- geographic focus;
- article-length limits;
- desired journal tier;
- turnaround preference only when official turnaround information exists.

When a journal is an excellent fit but violates a constraint, do not silently drop it. Show it as `EXCLUDED BY AUTHOR CONSTRAINT` and state why.

## 11. Output format

Begin with a compact table:

| Rank | Journal | Fit | Why it fits | Submission fee | Other charges | Main requirement risk | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then provide detailed profiles for Tier A and Tier B.

End with:

### Submission sequence
1. [Journal] - reason.
2. [Journal] - reason.
3. [Journal] - reason.

### Before submitting to Journal #1
List only the journal-specific actions not already captured by the general readiness blockers.

### Verification limits
List every fee, policy, or requirement that could not be verified.

## 12. Non-negotiable rules

- Current web research is mandatory for current fees and submission requirements.
- Official journal/publisher sources dominate secondary summaries.
- Never invent a fee, APC, word limit, review model, policy, ranking, acceptance rate, or turnaround time.
- Never infer `$0` from silence.
- Distinguish intellectual fit from prestige.
- Inspect recent actual publications, not only aims/scope.
- Do not hide an awkward recent precedent that weakens a novelty claim.
- Do not recommend a journal that does not accept the manuscript type.
- Do not present a conditional target as an immediate recommendation when the paper is not ready.
